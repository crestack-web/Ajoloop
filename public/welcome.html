Welcome

Nobody should face a hard season alone.

Ajo Loop is a place to belong. We are building it on an old African idea: ordinary people looking after each other, taking turns, and showing up when life gets heavy. Start with friends, find your community, and let trust grow from there.

Where this comes from

Ajo is the Yoruba name for a rotating savings circle. Versions of it exist all over Africa and beyond. Nobody wrote the early history down, so the dates below are broad, and practice has always varied from town to town.

  1. Long before banksDates uncertain

    Neighbours start pooling

    Across the continent, people gathered small sums on a schedule and gave the whole amount to one member at a time. There was no paperwork. Kinship, market reputation and the fear of letting down people you see every day did the work of a contract.

  2. Yoruba landEsusu and the alajo

    A trusted collector

    Among the Yoruba, groups called esusu met on a fixed rhythm. A respected collector, the alajo, gathered contributions daily or weekly and made sure each person's turn came. Being a good alajo meant being known as someone whose word held.

  3. The colonial periodLate 1800s to mid 1900s

    Banks that did not fit

    In many places the formal banks served trade for export and asked for collateral or papers that traders, farmers and women often did not have. Savings circles kept running alongside them and quietly financed stalls, school fees, weddings and funerals.

  4. Across the AtlanticCarried by people

    The idea travels

    Africans who were enslaved, and later those who migrated, took the practice with them. It lives on in the Caribbean as sou-sou, susu and partner, and in diaspora communities around the world.

  5. 1950sWritten down

    Scholars take notice

    The anthropologist William Bascom published a well-known study of Yoruba esusu in 1952. It is one of the early written accounts of something millions of people already took for granted.

  6. TodayStill going

    Old trust, new tools

    Market women, drivers, teachers and neighbours still run circles, now next to cooperatives, microfinance banks and phone apps. The weak point has not changed: trust. A collector who disappears can end a circle and a friendship. That is why clear records and real consent matter.

The same arrangement has been named in almost every language on the continent. A few of them:

  • IsusuIgbo, Nigeria

  • AjoWhere we start

Rotating savings circles are not only African. Similar groups exist in Asia and Latin America under other names. Africa is where this one comes from for us.

Our mission

To help people stay rooted in community, with real friends and neighbours who support them through the hardest parts of living.

A hospital bill that arrives without warning. School fees due on Monday. A shop that needs restocking. A wedding you cannot afford to miss. Hardship is lighter when someone knows your name and picks up the phone. Money circles worked for centuries because of that, and the money was never the whole of it.

Belong first

Anyone can start a group for friends, a street, a trade or a shared interest. Joining is free and asks nothing of you. A group is about people, not payments.

Show up

Support is mostly attention: a call, a seat at the table, a hand with a heavy week. Trust grows from small things done again and again, and we want to make those easy to see.

Choose with open eyes

A savings circle is a separate, optional step with its own terms. You read them and say yes yourself. Being in a group never puts you in one.

One world, five places

Ajo Loop is a social life game first. You build a life in Kano City, meet people, and find where you belong. Each part has a job.

  • Life

    Gives you an identity: your character, your daily needs, your name in the neighbourhood.

  • Town

    Gives you somewhere to go: the market, the workplace, the park, the Suya Spot.

  • People

    Gives you someone to meet, and a reason to keep showing up for them.

  • Groups

    Gives you somewhere to belong, public or private, started by anyone.

  • Ajo

    Gives you an optional way to take part in rotating savings, kept apart from everything else.Optional

What we hold ourselves to

Trust is the whole product, so we start with limits on ourselves.

  • Game money is game money. It is never mixed with real naira.
  • Joining or leaving a group never creates, changes or cancels a financial promise.
  • Every savings circle has its own members, written terms and records, and nobody joins without saying yes.
  • New people are not treated as untrustworthy. Everyone starts from a fair place.
  • Private groups stay private, and your public profile shows only what you choose.

The loop was always the people.

Generations before us built trust one turn at a time. Ajo Loop is a place for that to keep happening, among friends, in a community that has your back.
